CASE STUDY 01 · COMMUNITY MANAGEMENT

Kytisa Legal

Community Manager Intern · Casablanca · Jan – Apr 2026
OVERVIEW

Kytisa Legal is a legal-services brand that needed a digital voice. I built and ran its social presence end to end — content planning, design, publishing and community engagement — turning a dormant page into an active channel that educates its audience while protecting a professional, trustworthy image.

THE CHALLENGE

Legal content is dense and easy to get wrong. Engagement had to grow without ever sounding casual, promotional or imprecise — credibility was non-negotiable.

MY ROLE

Sole community manager: content strategy, copywriting, visual design, publishing calendar, community replies and monthly performance reporting.

Approach

1
Audience-focused content pillars

Mapped what the audience actually searches for — contract questions, employment rights, OHADA rules — and built recurring content pillars around those needs instead of firm news.

2
A consistent visual system

Locked a single template system in the brand palette so every post is recognisable at thumbnail size, across posters, carousels and quiz formats.

3
Educational formats over promotion

Turned legal knowledge into carousels and quiz posts that invite an answer, so the audience learns something and has a reason to comment.

4
Optimised publishing rhythm

Tested posting days and times against reach, then settled on a schedule that tripled output without diluting quality.

CASE STUDY 03 · BRAND IDENTITY

Atlas Cinematography Club

Founder · Université Hassan II, FSJES Ain Chock
OVERVIEW

I founded Atlas to give film-minded students a place to make work together — then gave it the identity of a real organisation. Brand guidelines, communication materials and recruitment campaigns took it from an idea to an officially accredited club at FSJES Ain Chock.

THE CHALLENGE

Build recognition for something that did not exist yet — with no audience, no budget and no visual assets to inherit.

MY ROLE

Founder: brand identity, guidelines, recruitment campaign, communication materials, and organising the club's creative initiatives.

Approach

1
Values first

Wrote the club around four core values — Creativity, Synergy, Critical Thinking and Freedom of Expression — and let every decision after that answer to them.

2
A cinematic identity system

Documented logo use, typography hierarchy and a crimson-on-black palette in a full brand guidelines deck, so anyone in the club could produce on-brand material.

3
Recruitment as a campaign

Treated the first intake like a launch: posters, social announcements and a clear call to join, sized for campus walls and phone screens alike.

4
Official accreditation

Presented the club to the faculty and secured its place among the accredited student clubs of FSJES Ain Chock.

CASE STUDY 04 · FREELANCE

LegalTech Video Content

Freelance · Video editing & motion
OVERVIEW

A freelance engagement producing video content for a legal-technology product: a short promotional film to introduce the product, and a longer demo film walking through how it works. Both were cut for two contexts — vertical for social feeds, wide for the website.

THE BRIEF

Explain a technical legal product to a non-technical audience in the seconds before they scroll past, without oversimplifying what it does.

MY ROLE

Freelance video editor: structure, pacing, motion, on-screen text and export for each destination format.

Approach

1
Lead with the problem

Both films open on the friction the product removes, so the viewer recognises their own situation before any feature appears.

2
Show, do not describe

The demo film follows the actual interface in sequence — every claim is visible on screen rather than narrated over a stock shot.

3
Pacing built for the feed

Cut tight with on-screen text carrying the message, so the promo works muted and autoplaying as well as it does with sound.

4
One edit, two formats

Framed every shot so the same edit exports vertically for social and wide for the site with no reshoot and no crop damage.

CASE STUDY 05 · FREELANCE · POSTER DESIGN

National Disability Awareness Campaign

Freelance via Fido · DSSC, FSJES Ain Chock & Entraide Nationale · June 2025
OVERVIEW

A freelance engagement through Fido: design the launch poster for Morocco's first national disability awareness campaign, and photograph the conference that opened it. The campaign is a partnership between the Laboratory of Law, Society and Comparative Systems (DSSC) at FSJES Ain Chock, Université Hassan II, and the Regional Coordination of Entraide Nationale for Casablanca-Settat, built around a study day at Les Cliniques de Droit on how scientific research can serve legal awareness for people in situations of disability.

THE CHALLENGE

One poster carrying four institutional identities, an Arabic headline and full event details — formal enough for the partners, clear enough to read at a glance.

MY WORK

Poster design and conference photography. Scope stopped there by design — the campaign's content and programme were the partners' own.

Approach

1
Institutional hierarchy first

Four partner identities share the header — kingdom, faculty, national mutual aid and research laboratory — ordered so no partner reads as subordinate to another.

2
Dignity in the imagery

Illustration showing people with a range of disabilities together and active, rather than isolated or clinical.

3
Arabic-first typography

Headline set in Arabic with generous line spacing, so the campaign title stays readable at poster distance and on a phone screen.

4
Photographed the conference

Covered the study day itself, giving the partners a set of images to document and share the event afterwards.

The campaign poster

Conference photography

Coverage of the study day at Les Cliniques de Droit, FSJES Ain Chock — sessions and partner group photos for the organisers to document and share the event.

CASE STUDY 06 · PODCAST PRODUCTION

La Clinique du Droit de Casablanca

Marketing & Content Team · Jan – Sep 2025
OVERVIEW

La Clinique du Droit de Casablanca offers free legal resources to students and the public. On its marketing and content team I produced a run of seven short podcast episodes built to support law students through their final-project (PFE) research, and set the content strategy that got the clinic’s free services in front of the students who needed them.

THE CHALLENGE

Students were getting stuck at the same points in their PFE research, and the clinic’s free help was going unused simply because too few people knew it existed.

MY ROLE

Recording, direction and post-production across all seven episodes, plus the content strategy promoting the clinic’s resources to a student audience.

Approach

1
One question per episode

Each episode answers a single recurring PFE question, so a student can find the one they need instead of sitting through a full lecture.

2
Short by design

Kept episodes short and tightly cut — the format had to fit between classes to actually get listened to.

3
Directed for non-broadcasters

Guided academic contributors through recording so the delivery stays natural and the substance survives the edit.

4
Content strategy around the series

Used the episodes as the entry point to the clinic’s free legal resources, giving the promotion something genuinely useful to lead with.
